How Jason Colapietro approaches small-business web development in Jupiter and Palm Beach County

Fast pages, plain answers and receipts for every claim. The working method behind Suede AI's small-business builds in South Florida.

By Jason Colapietro, Suede AI, West Palm Beach, Florida

A Jupiter business owner usually does not need a bigger website. They need one that answers the three questions customers ask on the phone, loads before the customer gives up in a parking lot, and shows up when someone asks an AI assistant for a recommendation. That is the whole brief Jason Colapietro works from, and it is less glamorous than most agency pitches.

Here is how Jason, founder and CEO of Suede AI in West Palm Beach, approaches small-business web development for shops, contractors and studios across Palm Beach County, using the Blueline surf concept (an independent, uncommissioned concept) as the running example.

Start from the counter, not the sitemap

Before any design, Jason Colapietro asks the owner to list the questions staff answer most often. For a surf shop it is conditions, rental availability and lesson ages. For a roofer it is storm damage, permits and financing. Those questions become pages, in the order customers ask them. Everything else, the about page, the gallery, the blog, comes after.

This is why the Blueline concept puts a live Juno Pier conditions panel above the catalog. It is not a gimmick. It is the first thing a surfer asks.

Build small and fast, then stop

Most small-business sites in Palm Beach County do not need a framework, a database or a monthly plugin bill. Suede AI usually ships static HTML with a small amount of JavaScript for the live pieces, hosted on a CDN. Pages load quickly on a phone at the beach, nothing breaks when a plugin updates, and the owner is not locked into anything.

The honest tradeoff: if a shop already runs inventory through Shopify or bookings through a scheduling platform, Jason will usually recommend keeping it and building the content layer around it. Rebuilding working systems for the sake of a portfolio piece is a cost the client pays, not the agency. Suede AI would rather spend that budget on content that brings in bookings.

Engineering habits from open source

Jason Colapietro came to local web work as an engineer first. He has 54 pull requests merged across 46 open-source repositories he does not maintain, including Jest, Adobe React Spectrum, Backstage, Hardhat and the Polkadot SDK. Getting code accepted into projects like those teaches habits that matter on a five-page site too: small changes, readable diffs, tests that prove the thing works, and no surprises for whoever maintains it next.

He has also shipped 9 iOS apps under his own Apple developer account and written five books available on Amazon, including The Claude Code Bible and Codex in Production, both about building software with AI coding agents. That is the toolset Suede AI uses day to day across client work, with human review on every change that ships.

Write for people, structure for machines

At Suede, every page gets a plain two-sentence answer at the top, then detail. Behind the copy, Suede AI adds structured data that describes the business, the page and its author, and keeps names, addresses and hours identical everywhere they appear. That consistency is most of what local SEO and generative engine optimization (GEO) come down to for a small business.

Jason keeps his own skills current: 21 earned course credentials across eight providers, including Semrush Local SEO Essentials and nine Claude Academy badges, plus 17 Microsoft Learn achievements with two learning-path trophies in building AI agents and generative AI apps on Azure. All of them are in the training credentials gallery.

Small-business websites are full of claims nobody can check: "award-winning," "five-star," "best in Jupiter." Jason Colapietro's rule is that every claim on a client site links to its proof, or it does not go on the page. Suede AI holds itself to the same rule with a public evidence page.

Measure where customers actually look

After launch, the Suede team tracks booked calls, form fills and reservations, not just traffic. Each month it also checks how the business appears in AI answers for a short panel of local prompts, such as "best paddleboard rental near Jupiter Inlet." The Agentix grader and Suede Scan help spot technical gaps that keep a page out of those answers.

Local context matters too. Jupiter Social, a local magazine for Jupiter, Florida, is published with Suede AI, which keeps the team close to how residents actually talk about the town, its beaches and its businesses.

When Jason says no

Not every business needs a custom build, and Suede's best advice is sometimes to change nothing. If a site already answers its customers' questions, loads quickly and ranks for its town, Jason Colapietro will say so and suggest small fixes instead. If the real problem is a missing Google Business Profile or unanswered reviews, that comes before any redesign.
